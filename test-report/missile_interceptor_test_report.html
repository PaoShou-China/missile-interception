0Summary and conclusions

This report tests the missile_intercept simulation system at four levels: unit/component, numerical and model verification, aerodynamic cross-check, and a 1000-case full-flow Monte Carlo (6DOF, with navigation/seeker/wind/datalink noise all enabled). Every test can be reproduced with the repository commands.

What passed
All 69 pytest cases pass; 5 formal numerical verifications pass; the Numba and pure-Python hot paths agree; aerodynamic anchors, DATCOM reproduction and the M>5 shock-expansion table are all within tolerance; the nominal single shot misses by 6.17 m; all 1000 cases complete without crashes.
Issues requiring attention
① P0: with sensor_noise_scale=0 the target-tracking covariance collapses to zero and the second datalink update raises LinAlgError('Singular matrix'), which makes main.py --converge and the error-budget ablation unusable.
② P1: a single-frame false lock can trigger a forced handover at 35.7 km, causing a kilometre-scale miss.
③ P1: about 23.5% of samples never complete handover. The main causes are the mismatch between the real acquisition window (IIR 25 km / radar 8 km) and the 45 km handover design, the 0.2 s hard dwell missing the window, and midcourse energy/attitude saturation.

Recommended priority actions

  1. For zero-noise filtering use a covariance floor or the Joseph form, or skip the update and keep the covariance positive definite when noise is zero.
  2. For forced handover add a "lock range ≤ physical acquisition range" check and a consistency check over at least 3 consecutive frames.
  3. Align the handover range with the seeker acquisition range; make the dwell time adaptive to t_go/closure rate so that the only window at 3 km/s is not missed.
  4. Add angle-of-attack / load-factor rate and energy constraints to midcourse guidance so that 35° angle-of-attack saturation at Ma 9 does not cause energy collapse.

2.1 Key numerical findings

  • State and coordinates: the packing layout shares a hash with the Numba kernel; the quaternion norm error after 2000 s / 40k steps is 0; the LLH/quaternion round-trip error is of order 1e-9.
  • Hot-path equivalence: atmosphere, the 14-dimensional missile derivative, the 6-dimensional target derivative, the autopilot and the fin mixer agree to a relative error ≤1e-10 between Numba and Python on random states.
  • Sensors: KF error <100 m within 20 s; locks are rejected outside the FOV and lost inside the 200 m blind range; angular glint grows as range decreases; noise streams are bit-reproducible for the same seed.
  • Control: second-order filter overshoot <25%; fin deflection ≤25° with rate ≤150°/s; saturated integrators freeze; q<100 Pa protection; gain scheduling bounded at both ends.
  • Guidance/handover: all bad geometries rejected; 0.2 s dwell + 0.5 s blending; Bezier endpoints/tangents correct; terminal guidance ≤40 g.

All component tests pass, but L4 exposed problems that only appear with closed loop + noise + random dispersion: singular zero-noise filtering (P0) and false locks triggering forced handover (P1).

6.1 Evidence chain for a false lock triggering forced handover

  1. Seeker physical acquisition: radar 45000×(0.005/5)^0.25≈8.0 km; IIR 25 km; the dual-mode takes the longer of the two, so the real upper limit is 25 km.
  2. Case 4 reports is_locked=True at 35.7 km, which cannot come from a real echo; the false-detection rate is 0.01/s and a single frame is enough to set it.
  3. Forced handover only requires dist≤38 km, speed, lead angle, FOV and seeker_lock, and does not check the lock range or the number of consecutive frames; a single-frame false lock is sufficient.
  4. The false detection deflects the LOS by about 0.35×FOV (≈7°), which corresponds to about 4.4 km of lateral error at 35.7 km; terminal guidance then flies along the wrong line of sight and misses by 3.69 km.

This is the end-to-end logic defect most worth fixing first: a sensor false detection is treated as a genuine acquisition by the handover state machine. Forced handover should require at least 3 consecutive frames, a lock range ≤110% of the physical acquisition range, and a consistency check using the tracker innovation covariance.

6.2 Two mechanisms for missing handover

  • State machine misses the window (case 2): at R≈25.7 km all thresholds were satisfied simultaneously, but the 0.2 s dwell was not completed and the lead angle rose to 13.1° 0.04 s later, resetting the counter; the geometry then deteriorated and the missile flew past at 5.76 km. The underlying issue is that the hard dwell does not match the short window at a 3 km/s closure rate, and the force branch is not effective when all_ok holds only instantaneously.
  • Energy/attitude collapse (case 28): from about 350 s the angle of attack saturates at -35°, Mach drops from 8.97 to 6.58 within 10 s and speed from 3009 to 737 m/s within 40 s; after fuel exhaustion the missile hits the ground 785 km from the target. This shows that midcourse guidance can issue extreme commands under strong dispersion, and that the high dynamic pressure at Ma 9 turns a small angle-of-attack error into enormous drag.

6.3 P0 defect: singular matrix on the zero-noise path

The error-budget ablations no_sensor_noise / truth_nav all fail; the single-shot reproduction stack is:

sensors/target_tracker.py:118 → K = np.linalg.solve(S, self.P).T
numpy.linalg.LinAlgError: Singular matrix (t≈2 s, second datalink update)

Root cause: in update_position_velocity, R=diag([σp²,σv²]); when noise_scale=0, σ=0, so the first update projects P exactly to the zero matrix and the second update has S=0 → singular. The seeker zero-noise update (Rm=0) has the same problem.

Impact: the noise-free ablations of main.py --converge and tools/miss_budget.py are unusable; the default 1000 cases with noise enabled and the nominal simulation are unaffected.

6.4 Engineering runtime issues

  • 24-process OOM: when 24 workers trigger Numba/LLVM and OpenBLAS simultaneously, LLVM/OpenBLAS memory exhaustion occurs on the 15.7 GB machine; 12 processes are stable. Recommended: workers ≤ min(12, cpu/2) with BLAS threads preset to 1.
  • Checkpoint-resume semantics: failed records are also written to the JSONL file, and --resume skips them by case_id without retrying; filtering out records with a non-empty error is recommended.
  • basic telemetry: all extended observables are still computed and then truncated, so CPU time does not drop; branching on profile inside _log is recommended.

7Conclusions and recommendations

  1. Basic software quality is good: 69 unit/component tests, 5 numerical verifications, Numba/Python equivalence, aerodynamic anchors and DATCOM reproduction all pass; the nominal miss distance is 6.17 m.
  2. All 1000 MC cases completed; the 10 m hit rate is 18.2% and the 50 m hit rate is 35.3%, with CEP50/CEP90 = 292.2/6981.9 m.
  3. The accuracy bottleneck is not terminal guidance itself: the median miss distance of completed handovers is 90.6 m, while 23.5% of samples never hand over and account for the vast majority of kilometre-scale failures.
  4. One P0 (zero-noise singularity) and two P1 issues (false-lock handover; handover/acquisition mismatch plus midcourse energy saturation) were located, together with suggested fixes.

7.1 Suggested follow-up testing

  • After fixing P0, rerun the full --converge and miss_budget matrix to quantify numerical error and the contribution of each noise source.
  • After fixing P1, run an A/B regression on the same seed set, comparing hit rate, no-handover rate and CEP90.
  • Add fault injection for false lock / dropped frames / datalink interruption, and add a sensitivity sweep over the 25–45 km handover window.
  • If mission success is defined by lethal radius, re-evaluate at 50 m or the actual warhead radius and report CEP alongside it.
